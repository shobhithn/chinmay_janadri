Understanding GST E-Invoicing: Who Needs It?

02 Aug 2026
6 min read
Mandaknalli Janadri & Co.

E-invoicing has moved from "something only large corporates worry about" to a compliance requirement that now touches a growing share of small and mid-sized businesses in India. Here's what it actually is, who needs to comply, and how to get ready without last-minute panic.

What Is GST E-Invoicing?

E-invoicing under GST doesn't mean generating your invoice on a new piece of software. It means that specified businesses must report their B2B invoices to the Invoice Registration Portal (IRP), which validates the invoice and returns a unique Invoice Reference Number (IRN) along with a digitally signed QR code. That IRN and QR code then have to appear on the invoice you actually hand to your customer.

The point of the system is simple: it lets the GST Network cross-check what a seller has reported against what a buyer later claims as input tax credit — in real time, rather than months later during an audit.

In short

E-invoicing is a reporting and validation step that happens before you issue an invoice — not a replacement for your existing billing software.

Who Needs to Comply?

Applicability is based on aggregate annual turnover in any preceding financial year since GST rollout — and the GST Council has progressively lowered this threshold over successive notifications, bringing more businesses into scope each year. If your turnover has ever crossed the applicable threshold in any year, e-invoicing continues to apply going forward, even if turnover later drops.

Because the threshold has changed more than once, the safest approach is not to rely on a number you read somewhere online — confirm your current applicability with us based on your turnover history, rather than assuming last year's rule still holds.

  • E-invoicing applies to B2B supplies, exports, and B2B credit/debit notes — not to B2C retail invoices.
  • Once applicable, it applies to all GSTINs under the same PAN, not just the branch that crossed the threshold.
  • A handful of sectors — including banks, NBFCs, insurers, GTAs and passenger transport services — are specifically exempted regardless of turnover.

How the Process Actually Works

In practice, once you're in scope, the workflow looks like this:

  1. You generate the invoice in your normal accounting or billing software (Tally, Zoho, or similar).
  2. The invoice data (JSON) is pushed to the IRP — either directly, via a GST Suvidha Provider, or through your accounting software's built-in integration.
  3. The IRP validates the data, checks for duplicates, and returns an IRN and signed QR code.
  4. You print or embed that IRN and QR code on the invoice before sending it to your customer.
  5. The invoice data flows automatically into your GSTR-1, reducing manual return-filing work.

A Short Readiness Checklist

Before your applicability date arrives, it's worth working through this list rather than reacting to it after the fact:

  • Confirm whether your turnover history actually brings you into scope this year.
  • Check whether your billing software has a direct IRP integration, or whether you'll need a GSP.
  • Train whoever raises invoices — the IRN has to be generated before the invoice reaches the customer, not after.
  • Reconcile a few months of historical invoices against GSTR-1 to catch formatting issues early.

What Happens If You Get It Wrong?

Non-compliance is treated seriously

An invoice issued without a valid IRN, where e-invoicing applies, is not treated as a valid tax invoice at all — which can block your customer's input tax credit and expose you to penalties under Section 122 of the CGST Act.

The good news is that once the integration is set up correctly, e-invoicing mostly runs in the background — the real risk is in the first few weeks after a business crosses the threshold, before the process is bedded in.
